### Style Guide for Physiotutors Content Localization

#### Tone and Register
- **Professional yet Approachable**: The content should maintain a professional tone suitable for an educational platform while being approachable to engage a wide audience of physiotherapists, from students to seasoned professionals.
- **Encouraging and Motivational**: Use language that motivates and encourages users to engage with the content and improve their skills.
- **Informative and Clear**: Ensure that the information is presented clearly and concisely, avoiding jargon unless necessary, and providing explanations when technical terms are used.

#### Punctuation
- **Consistent Use of Commas and Periods**: Use commas to separate items in a list and periods to end complete sentences. Ensure punctuation is consistent throughout.
- **Colons for Introductions**: Use colons to introduce lists or explanations, especially in course descriptions or feature lists.
- **Quotation Marks for Titles**: Use quotation marks for course titles, app names, and other specific content references.

#### Preferred Constructions
- **Active Voice**: Use active voice to make the content more direct and engaging. For example, "Physiotutors offers a wide range of courses" instead of "A wide range of courses is offered by Physiotutors."
- **Second Person**: Address the reader directly using "you" to create a personal connection and encourage engagement.
- **Short Sentences**: Use short, clear sentences to improve readability and comprehension.

#### Formatting Conventions
- **Headings and Subheadings**: Use clear and descriptive headings and subheadings to organize content and guide the reader through the material.
- **Bullet Points for Lists**: Use bullet points for lists to enhance readability, especially when listing features, benefits, or course details.
- **Consistent Font and Size**: Ensure that the font and size are consistent across all content to maintain a professional appearance.

#### Voice
- **Authoritative and Trustworthy**: The voice should convey authority and trust, reflecting the expertise of Physiotutors and its contributors.
- **Friendly and Supportive**: While maintaining authority, the voice should also be friendly and supportive, encouraging users to engage with the content and seek further learning.

#### Notable Stylistic Patterns
- **Use of Numbers**: Present numbers consistently, using numerals for all numbers (e.g., "2 Million" instead of "two million") to ensure clarity and quick comprehension.
- **Capitalization for Emphasis**: Use capitalization sparingly for emphasis, such as in headings or key phrases (e.g., "HELPING YOU BECOME THE BEST PHYSIOTHERAPIST YOU CAN BE").
- **Consistent Terminology**: Use consistent terminology throughout the content, especially for technical terms related to physiotherapy and education.

#### Additional Considerations
- **Cultural Sensitivity**: Be mindful of cultural differences and ensure that content is appropriate for a global audience.
- **Localization of Currency and Dates**: Adapt currency and date formats to suit the target audience's locale, ensuring clarity and relevance.
- **Feedback and Iteration**: Encourage feedback from users to continuously improve the content and its localization.

This style guide aims to ensure that future translations of Physiotutors content maintain a consistent and professional standard, enhancing the learning experience for physiotherapists worldwide.
